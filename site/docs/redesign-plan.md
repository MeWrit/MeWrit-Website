# MeWriT website: redesign plan

Draft, 9 October 2026. For review by the client lead and Dr Hetal Shah before any build work starts. Built from a visitor's study of hubtown.co.in and oryzo.ai (public pages only, robots.txt honoured, nothing copied) and from the MeWriT deck (July 2026) and Dr Shah's CV (August 2026).

## 1. What the references actually do

**Hubtown**
- The home page is only a film: six named chapters (Future, Innovation, Collaboration, Excellence, Purpose, Legacy) listed in a rail on the left.
- Each chapter puts one uppercase line on screen (about 6 words), one short supporting paragraph (about 25 words) and one link to an inner page. Nothing else.
- One world, travelled through: a glowing cube over water between mountains, a canyon with a river of light, rising streams, buildings on a plain, a low flight over lit terrain, a city in a valley at night. The object transforms (cube to stream to buildings to city) and the setting changes completely behind it, chapter by chapter.
- The last chapter doubles as the footer (address, social links).
- Inner pages (About, Projects, Careers, Contact) use the same system: a full-screen headline over that page's own scene, then sparse blocks over the same world: one big statement, small data cards, counters, numbered items (001 Purpose, 002 Growth), a form over the 3D object.
- The same chrome on every page: logo, five links, a menu, a sound toggle, "Scroll to explore", a chat link, a thin frame.

**Oryzo**
- One product film with no sections: 0 to 26 words per frame, usually under 15. Single huge words or short phrases carry the chapters.
- One hero object throughout; every chapter changes the whole look: photographic, a hand-held moment, a neon "AI" mode, a gallery of framed photographs, a magazine cover, a real desk scene, a thermal-camera view, blueprint line drawings, macro texture.

**What this means for us.** Both are one system from the first frame to the last: a single hero object, a world that changes behind it, very few words at a time, one action per chapter, and all the detail on inner pages built in the same language. The current /experience/ page fails on exactly these points: it bolts home-page sections onto a 3D film, every scene carries a paragraph, a list, labels and a figures panel, and only the particle shape changes while the world stays the same.

## 2. The concept: a document, read by lamplight

MeWriT's product is the written document, so the whole site is designed as one: a document you read at night.

- **The hero object is always the page.** One sheet of light that writes itself, turns, becomes a dossier, a journal article, a slide, a handout, a reviewed draft.
- **Night is for the film, paper is for reading.** The film plays on deep navy, with statements large enough to read on dark. Anything longer than a sentence appears on ivory paper with navy ink: inner-page content, forms, detail. This is the balance Dr Shah asked for (dark text on light for reading; the navy experience for atmosphere), and it is literally the medium she works in.
- **Worlds drawn as luminous engravings.** Each chapter's setting is drawn in fine lines with hatched shading, the style of old scientific and anatomical plates, lit by warm lamplight and cool moonlight: an archive, a reading room, an auditorium, a workshop room, a study, a night sky, a dawn horizon. These are real places where medical writing lives, not abstract tech space.
- **The interface is page furniture, not a sci-fi HUD.** Crop marks at the corners (print registration marks), a running head with the chapter's name, a folio (page number) at the foot, footnotes for sources, a contents list in the margin. The figures panel, readouts and decoding labels go.
- **One transition everywhere: the page turn.** Between chapters the page turns like a leaf and the next world appears behind it. Labels type in like a typewriter rather than decode like a terminal.
- **Typography:** statements in the serif (Fraunces), sentence case, editorial. Labels in IBM Plex Mono, small, like figure captions and document metadata. Body in Inter, used sparingly.
- **Colour:** navy night from the logo, ivory paper, orange ink (the logo's accent), gold for seals and highlights. Restrained glow; no neon.

**Why this does not read as a tech startup:** real settings instead of abstract space; editorial serif in sentence case instead of uppercase grotesque; page furniture instead of dashboards; warm lamplight against the navy; every figure footnoted to its source; the AI practice framed as human judgement; no hype words. It still moves like Hubtown: a continuous world, a transforming hero, one line at a time.

## 4. The system (applies to every page)

- **Chrome:** the logo on its white plate (the logo is never altered); five links: Practices (menu of the five), Academy, Publications, About, Contact; one button: Start a project. Phones: a full-screen menu with the same items and the five practices listed.
- **Page furniture:** crop marks, running head, folio, footnotes, a contents rail on long pages. The same on the home film and inner pages.
- **Night and paper:** films and page openings on night; reading on paper sheets that rise over the night, typeset like a document (numbered sections, margin notes for standards and sources).
- **Motion:** the page turn between chapters and when entering an inner page (the hero page grows to fill the screen and becomes the paper sheet); headlines rise line by line; labels type in; nothing glitches, scrambles or flashes.
- **Words at a time:** film chapters within the limits above; on inner pages, no more than about 60 words in view at once, in lists rather than paragraphs where possible.
- **Accessibility:** every chapter is real text in order; reduced motion swaps flight for fades; without WebGL the worlds become still engravings and the text still reads.

### 10.3 Moving between chapters

No page turn (rejected) and no particles (rejected, 10 October: "get rid of the particle thing, go as per the mock ups"). The page is drawn as on the design board: a sheet, the dossier with its tabs, the journal article, a slide, a handout, the letter, and on the record the star; in the study the draft is on the display, as a real editor. Within a chapter nothing moves: the camera holds still (no drift, sway or pointer parallax; "i hate the way the page wiggles"). Between chapters the page's ink loosens like ink in water and settles into its next form.

**One house, joined by its windows and doors** (chosen 10 October): the camera goes to the study's window as the light goes and looks up at the stars (on the record); comes down the moonbeam through the archive's skylight (regulatory writing); goes through the archive's far door into the reading room (scientific publications); at first light in the reading room's windows goes next door to the auditorium (medical communications), across the hall to the workshop room (training), back to the desk and its display (AI/ML advisory), the desk in the late afternoon (how we work), and out through the study's window to the horizon at sunset (correspondence). The light changes only three times (dusk, dawn, sunset), each as a slow change across the frame, never a flip.

**The camera really travels** (10 October: "it feels too 2D, the camera should pan through the window and pan up and then reveal the sky and stars"; then "the transitions and motions are not smooth and very flimsy and unpredictable and loads the scene before going out of the window"). How it is built:
- The study is laid out in depth: its drawing split into layers (the wall with its doorway and window open, what is seen through the doorway, the desk with everything flat on it, and the volumes, plant, display, lamp and coffee standing as cut-outs), each projected back onto its place from the eye that drew it, so from that eye the room is exactly the drawing and from anywhere else it has depth. The view out of the window is a wide backdrop well behind the wall.
- Every move is one smooth camera path (no jolt at any key, an even pace, eased at its ends). The next room is placed beyond the opening the camera leaves by, so the path runs on into it and ends at its rest, and the rooms change as the camera passes the opening. Through the study's window the night beyond is the real sky, seen only once the camera has gone out.
- The moves: to the window as the light goes, out through it and up to the stars; down from the stars into the archive past the moonbeam; down the aisle and through the far window into the reading room; dawn, then a turn to the right into the auditorium; in to the slide on the auditorium's screen, through it as its ink loosens, into the workshop room; a turn toward the door and back into the study, in to the display; out to the desk; out through the window at sunset, on over the town toward the horizon.
- The scroll is followed on a spring; a stop part way through a move is finished in the direction the reader was going. The record's star is drawn like the chart's own bright stars and stands in the middle of the sky.

### 11.2 Why the film felt that way (the diagnosis given)

- Line drawings with no surfaces: by day every room was thin ink lines on blank paper, a wireframe that looks unrendered.
- The study was a flat drawing cut into cardboard layers: each object a cut-out at one depth, so the desk clipped its base and it hovered above its own shadow.
- Six rooms built separately, in different styles and scales, joined by fades: the camera flew through nothing between them (the empty frames).
- The bounce: camera paths that swung past their turning points and came back, and a settle that glided the film on by itself when the reader stopped.
- Pieces with no meaning: the page floating in each room, the ink dissolve, the star. The sunset mismatch was two horizons: the view drawn in the window and the 3D world beyond it.
- Nothing moved unless the reader scrolled; the words (bottom left) and the subject (right) split the eye; the contents appeared twice.

### 11.3 What Hubtown does (its public site, walked through at 32 points with the mouse wheel)

- One continuous world (a dark landscape of water, terrain and light): the camera never leaves it, so every frame is full.
- One hero object performing in the centre: a glowing cube turns, breaks into paths of light, which become a network and then buildings in a valley. Every change happens inside the world and never fades to empty.
- The words centred over the scene, below the hero: a short headline in capitals, a few lines, one action; letters that resolve into place.
- One visual language from start to finish (navy, electric blue light, mist): the consistency is what makes it feel rich.
- Quiet furniture: a thin frame round the screen, a chapter list on the left (Future, Innovation, Collaboration, Excellence, Purpose, Legacy), a bottom bar (sound, scroll to explore, chat).
- Not to copy: its all-dark technology look. MeWriT keeps paper and ink and the day's light.

### 11.4 The decisions

- **Look: illustrated 3D.** Every object a real solid: thickness, softened edges, a proper base, contact shadows and soft occlusion where things meet. Lit by the window (the hour of the day) and the desk lamp. The palette: paper and ivory walls, warm wood, navy, muted bindings, the reviewer's orange and gold as accents. A fine navy ink line on silhouettes and creases, so it stays drawn, and a faint paper grain over the picture. It must read as finished at every frame.
- **World: one house, two connected spaces.** The study, and through its open doorway (visible from the first frame) the library hall: stacks of bound work, the archive table, reading tables with lamps, a reading stand, tall windows, a lectern with a screen at the far end, seminar tables with a whiteboard. The camera walks short, visible routes between places; every in-between frame is a real view of the house. One sky for the whole house, seen through every window; the day passes in it.
- **Layout: as Hubtown.** Each chapter's object performs in the centre of the screen; the words are centred over the scene below it (headline, one line, one action). The letterhead across the top; a slim chapter list on the left; the digital-layer cards placed around the object.
- **The contents: on the monitor.** The five practices are the editor's document on the display, real links, clickable (hover lights a line, a click takes the film there). The caption keeps only the headline and its line; no second list.

### 11.6 Motion

- The scroll is the film's clock: stop anywhere and the frame is complete and composed. No settle that moves the film on by itself.
- The scroll is followed with a light smoothing that never overshoots; camera paths that never overshoot (no swing to either side).
- Short, purposeful routes along visible ways (desk to display; display to the doorway; along the stacks; to the archive table; to the reading tables; to the lectern; back by the windows to the seminar tables; back through the doorway to the display; to the desk by the window).
- No dissolves. Things change physically: pages fan, stack, bind, open, slide, tick, fold and are sealed; the light changes in the windows.
- At rest the camera holds perfectly still; life comes from the places, not the camera: the lamp's glow, dust in the light, steam, the clock, a cursor typing, a comment arriving, a chart building, a checkbox ticking, the wax pressed.

### 11.7 How it is built

- three.js, built in code: the architecture, furniture and props modelled procedurally (softened boxes, turned profiles, tubes, extrusions), thousands of bindings as instances, free CC0 models only where they help (each licence checked and recorded).
- Light: the sun through the windows (direction and colour by the hour) with soft shadows; the sky's soft fill; the desk lamp and the reading lamps at night; ambient occlusion for contact; a gentle bloom on lamps only.
- The drawn character: an edge pass in navy (silhouettes and creases from depth and normals), a warm paper grade and grain.
- Text on screens and paper (the editor, the letter's links): real HTML laid onto the surface in perspective, so it stays sharp and clickable.
- The new film is built at its own address (/house/) beside /lamplight/, which stays as it is for comparison until the new one replaces it.
